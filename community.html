Urban Design

Strategic plans for new communities, city districts, and resort destinations.

Castillo Arquitectos creates master plans and urban frameworks for new communities, city districts, and resort destinations. The work combines strategic thinking with a precise understanding of how places function at the scale of the street — how people walk, gather, and build lives in them. Each plan is developed in close collaboration with clients, communities, and specialist consultants, and is designed to perform financially as well as architecturally.

  • New town & neighbourhood master plans
  • Urban infill & regeneration
  • Planned resort & tourist communities
  • Form-based codes & development regulations
  • Participatory design charrettes

Guatemala City, Guatemala

Antigua Pedrera

Urban Design — Master Plan + Architecture

The first neighbourhood of Ciudad La Pedrera — an ambitious 300-hectare master plan being developed on the former Cementos Progreso quarry, one of the most significant urban transformation projects in Guatemala City’s history. Castillo Arquitectos has served as local architect and general planning coordinator for over a decade, with master planning developed in collaboration with Gensler and urban design with Gehl.

Coatepeque, Guatemala

Nueva Miramar

Urban Design — Master Plan + Architecture, Phase 1 built

A master plan for more than 1,000 hectares conceived to transform an agricultural landscape into a complete, people-centred urban community. Developed in collaboration with Gehl Architects, uniting global urban design expertise with Castillo Arquitectos’ deep knowledge of the Guatemalan market and context. Phase 1 architecture — a commercial district, natural park, and sports centre — is now built and in use.

Comayagua, Honduras

Palmerola

Urban Design — Master Plan, with Gehl Architects

A 70-hectare mixed-use district adjacent to the new Palmerola International Airport. The master plan transforms an industrial zone into a walkable urban quarter organised around mobility hubs, public space, residential neighbourhoods, and light industry — capturing the economic energy of major infrastructure while remaining genuinely liveable.

Antiguo Cuscatlán, El Salvador

El Encanto

Urban Design — Master Plan + Architecture

El Salvador’s most exclusive residential golf community. Castillo Arquitectos led the master plan for the community’s residential expansion in an intensive five-day participatory design process with an international multidisciplinary team. The plan establishes a four-pillar strategy: mobility, public space, housing, and ecological landscape.

The buildings that give communities their identity.

Master planning is only the beginning. Castillo Arquitectos designs the buildings that give communities their identity — the market building that activates a plaza, the club house that anchors a neighbourhood, the mixed-use block that generates economic life at street level. This work grows directly from the firm’s urban design practice: the same place-based philosophy, applied at the scale of a building.

Curridabat, Costa Rica

Ciudad del Este

Architecture — Mixed-Use Commercial & Residential · Phases 1–3 complete, Phase 4 in design

One of the most enduring client relationships in Castillo Arquitectos’ history — a mixed-use development that has grown across multiple phases into a reference project for Costa Rica. Phase 1 challenged the dominant model of inward-facing, car-oriented commercial architecture, delivering a project that generates active street life and creates genuine public space. Phase 4 introduces a residential component, transforming the project into a true mixed-use district.

Guanacaste, Costa Rica

Las Catalinas — Plaza del Mercado & Los Altos Flats

Architecture — Mixed-Use & Residential within a Planned Community

Las Catalinas is a car-free coastal town on Costa Rica’s Pacific coast, internationally celebrated for its walkability and architectural quality. Castillo Arquitectos designed Plaza del Mercado — a mixed-use market building framing a civic plaza — and Los Altos Flats, a multi-unit residential building that steps up the hillside with layered terraces and ocean views. Both buildings respond to the town’s Mediterranean and Latin American colonial architectural language while each expressing its own character.
