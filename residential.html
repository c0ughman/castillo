Residential

Homes designed for remarkable places.

Shaped by site, climate, and craft — and built around the people who live in them.

Practice

Same rigour, different scale.

Castillo Arquitectos brings the same design rigour it applies to urban master plans to the scale of a single residence — from the organisation of rooms and outdoor spaces to the selection of materials, the proportioning of openings, and the design of custom joinery and details. The result is homes that feel of their place, and last.

Las Catalinas, Guanacaste, Costa Rica

Casa Los Monos

Custom House · 350 m²

A contrasting cream-and-brown palette references Guanacaste’s white-faced capuchin monkeys. An enfilade arrangement of the living and dining spaces gives every room a shared view to the Pacific Ocean, while the upper floor carves out a studio apartment for short-term rental. The traditional colonial courtyard is reinterpreted as a pool terrace.

Las Catalinas, Guanacaste, Costa Rica

Virginia

Custom House · 400 m²

Situated on a steep site in Las Catalinas’ Beach Town, Virginia rises as a gleaming white volume under the Costa Rican sun. Protective balconies and roofed terraces frame views from every level to the Pacific. Its vertical presence and restrained white facade give it a quiet monumentality — a landmark within the community without demanding attention.

Las Catalinas, Guanacaste, Costa Rica

Casa Curuba

Custom House · 360 m²

A courtyard house on a prominent corner site. The soft coral-orange of its walls — the colour of a curuba fruit milkshake — gives the house its warmth and its name. A large arched french door opens the main living space generously to the street, balancing privacy with engagement. Two interior courtyards create private outdoor rooms.

Las Catalinas, Guanacaste, Costa Rica

Menta

Custom House · 400 m²

Green — from the mint-tea colour of its walls to the unruly vines in its private garden — is integral to this corner house arranged around a central courtyard. Soaring levels permit extraordinary views to the turquoise waters of Costa Rica’s Pacific coast. The house combines the discipline of the courtyard plan with a vertical ambition that makes the most of its elevated position in the town.
